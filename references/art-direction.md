# Art direction: translate a mechanism, not a category

The reusable part of this skill is how it discovers truth and evaluates a film. The visual composition is authored for the product. Do not treat graph, storm, galaxy, globe, dashboard, or an existing example as a routing menu.

## The direction brief

Before building a scene, answer in plain language:

- **Who and why:** Who uses this, what problem or decision does it change, and what is distinctive in its domain? Read enough official docs, examples, or code to distinguish the product from similar tools.
- **Input → mechanism → output:** Identify the real objects being transformed. Avoid abstract verbs such as “innovates” or “empowers.”
- **Visual event:** What can move, split, connect, filter, accumulate, disappear, or become legible as the product acts? Show that action in the first second if possible.
- **Evidence:** Which on-screen facts have a source? Which visible objects are illustrative? A topology, weather field, benchmark, score, or result must never look measured unless its underlying data exists.
- **Alternatives:** Consider two distinct concepts with different spatial logic or motion, then choose by semantic fit and impact at phone size. Reject a concept if it relies on an attractive background to do the explanatory work.
- **Storyboard:** Specify 2–4 beats, with focal subject state, camera, light, peripheral evidence, and sound. A beat is a change in the product mechanism, not merely the next caption.

The brief can be short. It is a decision record, not a form to fill mechanically. If the source is insufficient to know the mechanism, mark that limitation and avoid specific claims.

## Review in motion

1. Hide captions and data panels. Can the changing focal image still suggest the product's action?
2. Reduce the film to a phone-feed width. Can the subject and decisive fact be read without zooming?
3. View three separated frames. Did relationships, structure, or state actually change? Then watch at normal speed to check whether the change is perceptible.
4. Compare with other films. If they share the same camera, layout, and reveal rhythm, the source-specific metaphor has not reached the composition.
5. Inspect every number and causal implication. Decorative bars and invented intermediate counts must not masquerade as data.

Do not solve a failed concept by only recoloring it, replacing a still image, adding particles, or moving the same HUD boxes. Reconsider the mechanism and the visible action first. Code primitives, render drivers, typography helpers, and evidence checks should be reused; authored shot composition should not.
